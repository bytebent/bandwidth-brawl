Bandwidth Brawl for macOS (AU + VST3, Apple Silicon and Intel)

Easiest way
  1. Right-click "Install.command" and choose Open (a plain double-click may be
     blocked the first time, because the script isn't from an identified developer).
  2. Click Open in the warning, and wait for "Installed" in the Terminal window.

Or by hand, in Terminal, from inside this folder:
  cp -R "Bandwidth Brawl.component" ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components/
  cp -R "Bandwidth Brawl.vst3" ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3/
  xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components/"Bandwidth Brawl.component" ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3/"Bandwidth Brawl.vst3"

Then in Ableton Live 11: Preferences > Plug-Ins > turn on "Use Audio Units"
and/or "Use VST3 Plug-In System Folders" > Rescan. It appears under the
manufacturer "Bytebent Audio". Use either the AU or the VST3, not both.

Setting up the sidechain (input B)
  1. Put Bandwidth Brawl on the track that should be input A.
  2. Open the plugin's device in Ableton and click the small triangle in its
     title bar to show the sidechain section. Turn Sidechain on and pick the
     track that should be input B in the "Audio From" menu.
  3. The "B" chip at the top of the plugin lights up once sidechain audio arrives.
  Without a sidechain, input B is silent and the plugin leaves input A alone at
  high budgets (a silent input never claims bandwidth).

"Bandwidth Brawl.app" is a standalone version for quick tests outside Ableton.

The xattr step is needed because the plugin isn't signed with a paid Apple
developer certificate; it removes the "downloaded from the internet" flag so
macOS lets Ableton load it.
